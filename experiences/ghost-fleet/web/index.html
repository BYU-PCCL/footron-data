01

You have seen this curve

The normal distribution — the bell curve — plotted over two axes instead of one. A hill, highest at the mean, tailing away in every direction.

02

Now look straight down it

Height has nowhere to go but into brightness. A soft bump of light, brightest at the centre, fading outward, never quite reaching zero. Gaussian is its other name.

03

It need not be round

Give it a different radius along each axis and it stretches into an ellipse, lying whichever way you turn it.

04

And it is not flat

Edge-on it almost disappears. Give it a third radius and it becomes an ellipsoid — a blob of light sitting in space.

05

It carries its own colour

Colour and opacity belong to the Gaussian itself. There is no texture, and no surface for a texture to sit on.

06

One is nothing

Alone it is a smudge. The whole idea is that you can pile thousands of them up and let them overlap.

07

Fifty-eight thousand

Pack enough together and the eye stops seeing blobs and starts seeing a surface. There is no surface — that is the trick.

08

Each its own colour

Oak and tar below the waterline, painted trim, gilding, weathered canvas aloft.

09

Together, a ship

Every plank, rope and sail is one of these. Which is why, when a cannonball lands, you can watch them come apart again.
