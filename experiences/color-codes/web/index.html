What Color
Is This?

A screen has no idea what red looks like. It has three lights per pixel, and a number for each one, and that is the whole of it.

Hex and rgb() are those same three numbers written two different ways — base 16 and base 10. hsl() is a different set of questions to ask about them, which is why it is the one you reach for when you want this color, but darker.

A new color every run, with the arithmetic drawn out. Pick up a phone to play instead: the wall shows a color, you pick its code.

Someone has a phone. The wall is showing a color and four codes — only one of them is the color on screen.

The idea

  • Three numbers, one byte each. Twenty-four bits is the whole color.
  • Hex is those same three bytes in base 16 — two digits per channel.
  • HSL reads hue, saturation and lightness off the biggest and smallest of the three.
